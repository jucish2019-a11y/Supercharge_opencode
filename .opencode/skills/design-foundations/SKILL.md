## What I do

I establish the foundational design decisions that must come before typography, color, or layout — the layer most AI tools skip entirely:

- **Purpose definition** — What the design must accomplish, for whom, under what constraints
- **Audience modeling** — Who will see this, what they expect, what they need
- **Aesthetic direction** — The visual personality that makes the result distinguishable from AI defaults
- **Constraint mapping** — Turn technical and content limitations into design opportunities
- **Decision documentation** — Record every foundational choice so later decisions can reference it

## When to use me

Use this skill when:
- Starting a new project, page, or feature that needs visual design
- A design feels generic or "AI-made" and you can't pinpoint why
- Choosing between aesthetic directions (minimal vs editorial vs brutalist etc.)
- Typography, color, and layout choices feel arbitrary or disconnected
- You need to defend design decisions to stakeholders

## How I work

### Checker mode (auditing existing design)

1. **Ask: Can you state the design's purpose in one sentence?** If not, the foundation is missing.
2. **Ask: Who is the primary audience?** If the design serves "everyone" it serves no one.
3. **Ask: What aesthetic direction was chosen, and why?** If the answer is "clean and modern," that's an AI default, not a direction.
4. **Ask: What constraints shaped this design?** If none were identified, the design is unconstrained — which means it defaults to safe and generic.
5. **Ask: Can every visual choice be traced back to a foundational decision?** If typography, color, and layout were chosen independently, they'll conflict.

### Applier mode (building from scratch)

1. **Define purpose** — One sentence. What must this design accomplish? Not "look good" — what specific outcome?
2. **Define audience** — Who will see this? What do they expect? What do they need? What do they distrust?
3. **Choose aesthetic direction** — Pick from real directions, not "clean and modern":
4. **Identify constraints** — Technical limits, content requirements, brand rules, accessibility needs.
5. **Document decisions** — Write them down. They become the criteria for every later choice.

## The three layers

Every design decision operates on three layers. They must reinforce each other or the design feels incoherent:

### Layer 1: Purpose

What the design must accomplish. This is not "look professional" — that's aesthetics. Purpose is:
- "Convince this specific persona to sign up"
- "Help this user complete this task in under 2 minutes"
- "Communicate that this product is trustworthy and precise"

If the purpose is vague, every later decision becomes arbitrary.

### Layer 2: Medium

Where the design lives. The same information design must adapt to:
- Screen vs print (Garamond is timeless on paper, blurry on screen)
- Mobile vs desktop (tap targets, line length, information density)
- Interactive vs static (states, transitions, feedback)
- Light vs dark mode (contrast inversions, color perception shifts)

Medium constraints are not problems — they are design opportunities. The Pantheon's coffers exist because the dome needed them. They're also beautiful.

### Layer 3: Aesthetics

The visual personality. Aesthetics without purpose is decoration. Aesthetics without medium awareness is impractical. But aesthetics that serve purpose and respect medium = design.

Aesthetic choices must be defensible: "I chose this typeface because it communicates tradition and authority, which serves our purpose of trust-building for a financial audience" — not "I chose it because it looks nice."

## Aesthetic directions (beyond "clean and modern")

Most AI output defaults to "clean and modern" — which now reads as AI-made. Choose a real direction:

| Direction | Qualities | Typefaces | Color behavior |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Editorial** | Authoritative, text-forward, structured | Serif headings, sans body | Restrained, single accent, high contrast B&W |
| **Brutalist** | Raw, confrontational, anti-decoration | Mono or system, large sizes | Limited palette, high contrast, stark |
| **Warm Craft** | Human, tactile, inviting | Humanist sans, slab serif | Earthy tones, warm neutrals, organic shapes |
| **Technical** | Precise, systematic, engineered | Geometric sans, mono for data | Cool neutrals, signal colors, grid-based |
| **Swiss** | Rational, grid-driven, objective | Grotesque sans, tight tracking | Red accent, black/white, no decoration |
| **Organic** | Flowing, natural, approachable | Rounded sans, humanist | Desaturated, analogous, rounded corners |
| **Retro** | Nostalgic, referential, period-specific | Period-appropriate pair | Era-appropriate palette |
| **Luxury** | Sparse, refined, high-status | Didone serif, thin sans | Metallics, deep neutrals, generous whitespace |

"The convergence problem" — every AI produces the same "clean, modern" output. That sameness is now a signal. Users register the generic feeling even when they can't name the patterns. Choosing a specific aesthetic direction is the first step toward design that doesn't look like AI made it.

## Decision documentation template

```
DESIGN FOUNDATIONS

Purpose:
  [One sentence: what this design must accomplish]

Audience:
  Primary: [Who, what they expect, what they need]
  Secondary: [Who else, with different expectations]

Aesthetic direction:
  [Chosen direction from the list above]
  [Why this direction serves the purpose]

Anti-directions (what we are NOT):
  - Not [direction] because [reason]
  - Not [direction] because [reason]

Constraints:
  - Technical: [browser support, performance, framework]
  - Content: [copy length, image assets, data density]
  - Brand: [existing palette, logo, voice]
  - Accessibility: [WCAG level, screen reader support, i18n]

Foundation-to-design mapping:
  - Purpose → [drives layout hierarchy, CTA placement]
  - Audience → [drives type size, density, interaction model]
  - Aesthetic → [drives typeface selection, color palette, spacing style]
  - Constraints → [drives technical implementation choices]

Decisions made:
  - Typography: [chosen] — because [mapped reason]
  - Color: [chosen] — because [mapped reason]
  - Layout: [chosen] — because [mapped reason]
  - Motion: [chosen] — because [mapped reason]
```

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Purpose stated in one clear sentence
- [ ] Primary audience identified with specific needs
- [ ] Aesthetic direction is specific (not "clean and modern")
- [ ] Anti-directions listed — you know what you're NOT doing
- [ ] Every aesthetic choice traces back to purpose, audience, or medium
- [ ] Constraints documented, not ignored
- [ ] Foundation-to-design mapping is explicit (not implied)
- [ ] At least one choice was initially counter-intuitive but serves the purpose

## Anti-patterns I avoid

- "Clean and modern" as an aesthetic direction — it's not a direction, it's the absence of one
- Choosing typography, color, and layout independently — they must be derived from the same foundation
- Skipping audience analysis because "it's for everyone" — design for everyone serves no one
- Treating constraints as problems — constraints are what make design design (without constraints, it's just art)
- Adding aesthetic choices that look nice but serve no purpose — every choice must reinforce the foundation
- Starting with visual preferences and working backwards to justify them — start with purpose, derive visuals
- Copying a reference design without understanding why it works — the reasoning matters more than the result
